Glossary

The book's key terms in alphabetical order, with a short definition of each and a link to the chapter where it appears.

Terms

4Ps
Product, price, place, promotion. Chapter 8
Affordable loss
The maximum money and time you accept losing in an experiment. Chapter 2
Brainstorming
Generating as many ideas as possible without immediate criticism. Chapter 3
Break-even point
The sales volume with neither profit nor loss. Chapter 9
Build–measure–learn
The testing loop of the lean startup method. Chapter 7
Business model
The logic of creating value, delivering it and earning from it. Chapter 6
CAC
The cost of acquiring one customer. Chapter 8
Calculated risk
Moving ahead after studying the possibilities and setting the acceptable loss. Chapter 2
Cash flow
The money actually coming in and going out over a period. Chapter 9
Commission model
Taking a share of each transaction between two sides through your platform. Chapter 6
Contribution margin
Selling price minus the variable cost per unit. Chapter 9
Creative destruction
New innovation replacing old products and methods (Schumpeter). Chapter 1
Creativity
Generating a new and useful idea. Chapter 4
Customer discovery
Testing a venture's hypotheses by talking directly with customers. Chapter 5
Design thinking
A human-centered problem-solving approach in five stages. Chapter 7
Dilution
The fall in the founders' ownership share as new investors join. Chapter 9
Disruptive innovation
A simple, cheap solution that starts in a neglected market and then displaces the major companies. Chapter 4
Early adopters
The group that tries new things early and influences others. Chapter 4
Early failure
Discovering an idea's flaw early and cheaply. Chapter 12
Effectuation
An approach that starts with the available means rather than a preset goal (Sarasvathy). Chapter 2
Elevator pitch
A pitch of under a minute that sums up the idea. Chapter 11
Entrepreneurship
Discovering an opportunity and turning it, through innovation, into value, by organizing resources and bearing calculated risk. Chapter 1
Founders' agreement
A written agreement setting out shares, roles and how disputes are resolved. Chapter 10
Franchising
Granting others the right to operate your model and brand for a fee. Chapter 12
Freelance document
A document that allows an individual to offer professional services legally. Chapter 10
Freemium
A free basic version and a premium paid version. Chapter 6
Growth mindset
The belief that abilities grow through effort and learning. Chapter 2
How might we…?
Framing the problem as an open question that invites solutions. Chapter 3
Hypothesis
A testable expectation about customer behavior. Chapter 7
Idle resource
An existing resource unused most of the time (a car, a room, equipment, free time), so it is cheap to put to use. Chapter 6
Innovation
Turning an idea into value that people actually use. Chapter 4
Intrapreneurship
Initiative and innovation from inside an organization, as an employee. Chapter 1
Job to be done
The goal for which the customer "hires" the product. Chapter 5
Limited liability company
A company in which the partners' liability is limited to their shares. Chapter 10
LTV
The value of what a customer pays over their whole relationship with the venture. Chapter 8
MVP
The simplest version that allows learning from real customers. Chapter 7
Net profit
What remains of revenue after deducting all costs and statutory obligations. Chapter 9
Opportunity
An idea with a real need, customers, the right timing and the ability to execute. Chapter 3
Persona
A description of a typical customer who represents the target segment. Chapter 5
Pitch
A short presentation aimed at persuading the listener to take a next step. Chapter 11
Pivot
A deliberate change in part of the model based on evidence. Chapter 7
Positioning
The image the customer holds of you compared with the alternatives. Chapter 8
Profit margin
Net profit ÷ revenue; it rises with sales because fixed costs are spread over more units. Chapter 9
Reckless growth
Expanding before the model is proven, or without a financial basis. Chapter 12
Scaling
Revenue growing faster than costs. Chapter 12
SCAMPER
A list of questions for developing ideas: substitute, combine, adapt, modify, put to another use, eliminate, reverse. Chapter 3
Small and medium enterprises
Businesses classified by number of employees or revenue; the backbone of the economy. Chapter 1
Social entrepreneurship
A venture whose first goal is solving a social problem while staying financially sustainable. Chapter 1
Start-up costs
Costs paid once when the venture begins. Chapter 9
STP
Segmentation, then targeting, then positioning. Chapter 8
SWOT
Analysis of strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats. Chapter 5
TAM / SAM / SOM
The total, serviceable and realistically obtainable market. Chapter 5
The 10/20/30 rule
Ten slides, twenty minutes, and type of at least 30 points. Chapter 11
Traction
What proves the idea works: interviews, trials, sales. Chapter 11
Trademark
The distinctive name or logo of a product, registered for protection. Chapter 10
Value pie
The difference between the most the customer will pay and the real cost of delivering the service, including time. Chapter 6
Value proposition
The reason the customer chooses you over the alternatives. Chapter 6
Win-win deal
A model from which the customer, entrepreneur, workers and partners all come away satisfied, with the customer paying an amount that rewards everyone. Chapter 6
