Guide to the book's tools

This guide brings the tools of all twenty chapters together in a single table, so that readers can return to it when they face a particular question. It does not reproduce the tools themselves; each appears, with its explanation, in its place in the relevant chapter.

Table T.1 The book's tools: the question each tool answers and the output it produces
ToolLocationThe question it answersOutput
Initiative classification cardChapter 1Is this initiative an innovation? What is its degree and its domain?A classification of the initiative that determines how it should be managed
External realities scanning matrixChapter 2Which external shifts call for a decision?A list of signals of change, each paired with decision questions
The leader’s mirrorChapter 3Do my leadership practices encourage innovation or hinder it?The gap between the leader’s self-assessment and the team’s assessment, with priorities for improvement
Innovation strategy on a single pageChapter 4Where do we look for value, with what ambition, and how do we succeed?A concise strategy ready for discussion and dissemination
System health checkChapter 5At which stage does the flow of initiatives break down?Identification of the stage that forms the bottleneck
Internal ecosystem mapChapter 6Who provides what, and how does an idea reach those who can help it?A map of units and their links, with gaps and duplications
The innovator's journeyChapter 7Where do ideas stall, and what did their owners need?The point on the path where the idea stalls, and the unmet need at that point
Revealing incidentsChapter 8What messages do people learn from events?A comparison of the actual messages with the stated values, and a proposed response
Team composition cardChapter 9Does the team have the roles and connections the stage requires?A team with a full set of roles and clearly identified gaps
The gate charterChapter 10Who decides, by what criteria, and when?A published charter for each gate
The problem cardChapter 11What is the problem, who lives with it, and why does it deserve solving?A problem statement endorsed by those who live with the problem
The concept cardChapter 12What is the candidate solution, and what assumptions does it rest on?A testable concept, with its assumptions ranked by risk
The value model canvasChapter 13How is the solution's value delivered, and how is it sustained?A value model, with assumptions to be tested in the experiment
The experiment cardChapter 14What are we testing, and how will we know the result?An experiment design, its result, and the decision that follows from it
Scaling readiness checkChapter 15Are the solution, the organization and the users ready to scale?A readiness rating for each dimension, and what is needed before or during scaling
Balanced measurement dashboardChapter 16Does the organization measure results, or does it settle for measuring activity?A balanced set of indicators that is hard to satisfy on paper alone
Value and total cost worksheetChapter 17What is the total cost over five years, and what is the value compared with the alternatives?A comparison of the proposal with two alternatives
Partner mapChapter 18Who provides what, and what do they want in return?A map of partners, identifying the primary source of risk
Responsibility check across the initiative journeyChapter 19Who might be harmed, and are we experimenting and using data responsibly?Responsibility questions answered at every stage
University innovation ecosystem diagnosticChapter 20Where does the university stand on each dimension of its ecosystem?A rating for each dimension, and two priority dimensions for action

The tools of Part Three form a single sequence for a given initiative: the problem card, then the concept card, then the value model canvas, then the experiment card, and finally the scaling readiness check. Around them sit the system-level tools, which give this path its environment, its decisions and its measurement.
